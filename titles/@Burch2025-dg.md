> [!abstract]
> Many students of industrial-organizational psychology may recall first hearing of Walter Van Dyke Bingham and his pioneering work in World War I with Walter Dill Scott, developing the first selection tests to be used in military assignments. However, diving below the surface reveals Bingham as a pioneer in merging the science of psychology with that of business, staunchly advocating for the applied focus of the field. Indeed, Bingham spent much of his career supporting the value and application of psychology to the “real world,” earning him the label, “the first academic entrepreneur.”
