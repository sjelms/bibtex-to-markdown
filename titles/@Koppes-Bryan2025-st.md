> Lillian Evelyn Moller Gilbreth (1878–1972) was a tireless advocate of applied psychology in the early part of the 20th century; asserting, researching, and practicing the applications of psychology that are at the forefront of current industrial-organizational (I-O) psychology. She was a consulting pioneer, held academic appointments, and received a plethora of awards and honors for her contributions. Because of her recognized expertise, she was frequently invited to serve on noteworthy committees appointed by all United States presidents from Hoover to Johnson. She exemplified the modern I-O psychologist by successfully blending research, practice, service, and a personal life.
