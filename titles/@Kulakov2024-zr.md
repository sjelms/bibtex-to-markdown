> [!bibliography]
> Kulakov, Alexandr. 2024. "An empirical study of the UK construction industry fragmentation, productivity, and performance." [[UCL Press]]. http://dx.doi.org/10.14324/000.th.10208357

> [!abstract]
> The UK construction industry is one of the key contributors to the wider economy, and yet this industry is perceived to have one of the lowest productivity levels and the slowest productivity growth. Despite decades of research, the drivers and determinants of the UK construction industry’s perceived relatively poor performance are still not fully understood and explained. This study considers what productivity is, how it is measured, what factors determine it and what may be done to improve it. The literature review covers research relating to productivity generally as well as construction-industry-specific research outlining the key challenges and recommendations made to date. The analysis is concerned with the problem of the fragmentation of the industry and business models employed by different types of construction enterprises. It uses the reported financial data of UK construction companies to quantify and analyse the fundamental characteristics of the UK construction industry, its fragmentation, productivity, and performance. The methods used lead to a range of representative datasets offering practically useful and up-to-date empirical benchmarks. Performance measurements from aggregate surveys and individual companies’ financial accounts supported this study’s purpose – namely, to understand and explain the productivity of different types and sizes of construction enterprises utilising a variety of business models. The findings broadly substantiate the standard economic theory of the firm (Coase, 1937) and offer insights into the factors that determine the productivity and financial performance of individual construction enterprises. The study contributes to academic knowledge about fragmentation, productivity, and performance of an industry, long seen as backward and idiosyncratic, but of strategic importance to the growth and prosperity of the economy as a whole, given its critical role in delivering, repairing and overall maintaining the nation’s infrastructure, housing, and commercial building stock (Meikle & de Valence, 2023). The empirically supported findings lead this study to conclude that adopting more of a “make” - more integrated, in-house production-oriented - business model is relatively more productive within any one of the observed segments and predominantly across all enterprise sizes of the UK construction industry. It also shows that there is broadly no correlation between enterprise size and productivity: the study finds no evidence to support the generally held belief that industry fragmentation affects productivity and, as a result, concludes that industry “fragmentation” is not a factor that is related to or is affecting productivity. It is shown that a higher proportion of total assets in an enterprise of nearly any size and type of activity, with a lower level of trade credit as a proxy for subcontracting (which together taken as an indication of a more of a “make” business model with relatively lower reliance on subcontracting) correlates with higher productivity. Therefore, it is concluded that greater integration - greater enterprise-level (and segment-level) in-house production - positively affects productivity. The study offers the insights gained from the empirical analysis of the fragmentation, productivity, and performance measurements of the “broad” construction industry and its segments, including construction-related professional services. The resulting set of up-to-date benchmarks will be useful for guiding and formulating policies, evaluating enterprise and investment performance relative to industry-wide as well as segment-specific metrics, informing further research and analysis, and developing new teaching materials.
