> Teicholz, Paul. 2013. "Labor-Productivity Declines in the Construction Industry: Causes and Remedies (Another Look)." https://www.aecbytes.com/viewpoint/2013/issue_67.html

> [!abstract]
> The article revisits labor-productivity trends in the U.S. construction industry from 1964 to 2012, examining the effects of BIM, lean processes, and the industry’s boom-bust cycle. It explains challenges in measuring productivity, including selecting output measures, price deflators, and labor inputs amid changing statistical methodologies. Using government data, it finds that construction labor productivity slightly declined over the period, contrasting sharply with substantial gains in nonfarm industries. Construction work hours fluctuated significantly, peaking during 2006–2007 before falling by about 26% by 2012. The article attributes stagnant productivity to unique project conditions, fragmented and competitive procurement practices, weak collaboration, and reliance on paper-based information and poorly coordinated teams.
