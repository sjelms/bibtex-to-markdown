> [!abstract]
> For a generation after World War II, American housing policy produced a reliable ladder from renter to homeowner. The rungs of that ladder have since collapsed—eroded by a broken housing production system, a private capital market that profits from scarcity over adequacy, and an affordable housing program where affordability expires. This paper argues that a presidential administration can act without new legislation to substantially expand the supply of affordable housing through a proven approach: community-owned housing. Markets ranging from Denver to central Florida to Salt Lake City have demonstrated that community-owned housing can expand affordable housing supply effectively and durably. The executive branch has the power to scale up community-owned housing through the Government-Sponsored Enterprises (GSEs—Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac), the Federal Home Loan Banks, state Housing Finance Agencies, and a new Community Ownership Capital Facility. Existing executive authorities provide the levers. What is needed is a president willing to pull them.
