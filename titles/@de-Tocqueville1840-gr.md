> Tocqueville, Alexis Charles Henri Clérel de. 1840. "Democracy in America." Vol. 2. [[Saunders and Otley]].

> [!abstract]
> This seminal work of political sociology and philosophy shifts focus from political institutions to the profound cultural, psychological, and social impacts of democracy on human nature and civil society. Volume Two examines how the "equality of conditions" shapes the inner lives of citizens, influencing their ideas, feelings, private customs, and intellectual habits. Alexis De Tocqueville introduces the concept of "individualism"—a democratic tendency to isolate oneself from the public sphere—and warns that it breeds civic apathy, leaving societies highly vulnerable to "democratic despotism." He observes that while citizens passionately pursue material well-being, this relentless drive often generates a pervasive spiritual restlessness. To counter these atomizing effects, de Tocqueville highlights the vital role of voluntary civic associations, a vibrant free press, and the uniquely American doctrine of "interest rightly understood," which connects personal advantage to the public good. He also explores democracy's impact on language, literature, gender roles, and family dynamics, concluding with a stark warning about the rising power of a centralized, bureaucratic state that threatens to gently stifle individual liberty under the guise of paternal care.
